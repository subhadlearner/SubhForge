# SubhForge v0.2.0 --- Architecture Fitness Review

## 1. Purpose

Before implementing SubhForge v0.2.0, re-evaluate the architecture from
first principles so that SubhForge can become dependable delivery
infrastructure for VidyaBeacon and the projects that follow.

The review is not intended to defend the current v0.2 design, nor to
redesign SubhForge for theoretical architectural purity. Its purpose is
to determine whether the current architecture provides the resilience,
correctness, recoverability, cost efficiency, bounded AI behaviour, and
operational simplicity required by a single developer relying heavily on
AI-assisted delivery.

The review must be evidence-driven. Where architectural reasoning alone
cannot establish confidence, use a focused architecture spike or fitness
test before accepting the decision.

### 1.1 Review Entry, Authority, and Exit

This document governs the Architecture Fitness Review that occurs **after
`stable_v0.1.1` has been delivered and accepted, and before the existing
v0.2.0 design/tracker is updated for implementation**.

**Entry condition**

- `stable_v0.1.1` is delivered and accepted.
- Planned v0.1.0/v0.1.1 hardening is closed or explicitly dispositioned.
- The current v0.2.0 design/tracker, implementation evidence, known failure
  modes, Kilo capabilities, and relevant industry practices are available as
  review inputs.

**Authority during the review**

The current v0.2.0 design is an **input and baseline to challenge, not an
authority that the review must preserve**. Existing decisions may be retained,
modified, removed, or replaced when the review evidence justifies doing so.

During the review:

- do not update the v0.2.0 design merely to keep it synchronized with evolving
  review thinking;
- capture proposed architectural decisions in the review output first;
- use isolated spikes/fitness tests only where evidence is needed;
- do not begin v0.2.0 production implementation from an architectural decision
  that has not yet been accepted.

**Exit condition**

The Architecture Fitness Review is complete only when:

- the review verdict is accepted (`KEEP`, `MODIFY`, or `REWORK`);
- accepted `KEEP` / `MODIFY` / `REMOVE` / `ADD` decisions are recorded with
  their reasoning and required validation;
- required architecture spikes/fitness tests have produced sufficient evidence;
- material impacts on the existing v0.2.0 design are identified; and
- the review is ready to hand off into the planned **v0.2.0 design
  reconciliation/update phase**.

Only after this exit is accepted should the v0.2.0 design/tracker be updated
and frozen for implementation.


## 2. Architecture Philosophy --- Borrow the Principle, Not the Enterprise Machinery

SubhForge should begin with established industry architecture and
engineering practices rather than inventing custom solutions by default.
Those practices must first be understood in terms of the problems they
solve, then deliberately adapted to SubhForge's actual environment: one
developer; AI-assisted engineering; strong reliability requirements;
cost and token sensitivity; low operational overhead; long-running
projects spanning many sessions; Kilo as the primary harness today; and
the need to recover projects even if SubhForge or Kilo itself fails.

For every architectural decision, ask:

> **What problem does the industry practice solve? → Do we have that
> problem? → What is the smallest robust implementation that solves it
> for Subhadeep + AI?**

Reliability should come from **putting determinism at the right
boundaries**, not from adding unnecessary components or enterprise
ceremony.

Therefore:

- adopt proven principles where they address credible SubhForge risks;
- simplify their implementation to our scale;
- avoid speculative abstractions and infrastructure;
- prefer deterministic software for mechanical correctness and bounded
  AI reasoning for semantic work;
- add complexity only when a demonstrated requirement, invariant,
  failure mode, portability concern, cost problem, or operational risk
  justifies it;
- do not reject a justified architectural improvement merely because it
  threatens the current delivery schedule.

This protects SubhForge from both fragile AI-heavy architecture and
fear-driven overengineering.

## 2.1 External Practice and Skill Reconnaissance

The review must actively look for credible existing implementations, skills,
framework patterns, official guidance, and engineering practices before
inventing a SubhForge-specific solution.

This is not a mandate to import external frameworks or skill packs. It is a
deliberate reconnaissance step intended to reduce reinvention and expose ideas
that have already been exercised by experienced practitioners.

For each material review case, ask:

> **Has a credible implementation already solved this problem? What problem
> does it solve, what assumptions does it make, what evidence supports it, and
> which principle — if any — should SubhForge adapt?**

When an external source is relevant, record:

1. the exact problem/capability being examined;
2. the source and version/commit where practical;
3. the mechanism or principle used;
4. the assumptions the source makes about workflow, authority, agents, tools,
   issue trackers, concurrency, persistence, or human involvement;
5. where those assumptions match or conflict with SubhForge;
6. the smallest useful idea we could adapt;
7. what should explicitly **not** be copied;
8. whether repository reasoning is enough or a focused spike is still needed.

Initial reference candidates include, but are not limited to:

- **Matt Pocock's `mattpocock/skills` engineering skills**, especially
  `wayfinder`, `codebase-design`, `domain-modeling`, `research`,
  `prototype`, `to-spec`, `to-tickets`, `tdd`,
  `diagnosing-bugs`, and `code-review`;
- **Addy Osmani's agent-skill work**, already used as source material for some
  current SubhForge skills;
- **Kilo's native commands, agents, skills, permissions, telemetry, and
  handoff capabilities**;
- **comparable spec-driven / work-item-driven engineering systems and
  issue-tracker-backed delivery workflows**, especially where they separate
  durable product authority from operational execution state;
- official/vendor guidance and mature open-source implementations relevant to
  the active architectural question;
- other credible engineering skill libraries or harnesses discovered during
  the review.

The list is intentionally open. The purpose is to compare alternatives and
choose the best fit for Subhadeep's workflow, not to standardize on one
external author or ecosystem.

### External-skill adoption rule

A third-party skill is treated like an engineering dependency, not trusted
prompt text.

Prefer:

`inspect → understand → pin source/version → adapt → record provenance → test`

over:

`install latest → inherit upstream behaviour automatically`

SubhForge may reuse an external skill unchanged only when its authority,
lifecycle, persistence, tool, and safety assumptions already match SubhForge.
Otherwise adapt the underlying principle into a SubhForge-owned skill or
workflow.

Existing SubhForge skill governance remains in force: project authority wins,
external provenance is recorded, and broad third-party packs are not imported
globally without review.

### Engineering-skill portfolio principle

The v0.2 review should be **more generous in discovering and evaluating
software-engineering skills**, especially reusable guidance that can improve
the quality of design before implementation begins.

Candidate capability families include:

- software/module/interface design;
- architecture and architectural trade-offs;
- domain modelling and terminology discipline;
- API, event and data-contract design;
- resilience, failure-mode and recovery design;
- testing architecture and testability;
- observability and operability;
- security and threat modelling;
- performance and cost engineering;
- migration and backward compatibility;
- refactoring and maintainability;
- research, prototyping and decision-making methods;
- code/spec/standards review.

This is **not** a target skill count. A capability may belong in a command,
agent, deterministic tool/MCP, project rule, or existing skill instead of a
new global skill.

Use the capability-first question:

> **What engineering capability does SubhForge need, and what is the smallest
> correct mechanism for supplying it?**

Only after that question is answered should the review ask whether a reusable
skill is the right mechanism.

### Skill admission gate

A candidate skill may be promoted into SubhForge only when the review records
sufficient evidence for all applicable checks:

1. **Need** — a real engineering capability gap or material quality benefit is
   identified.
2. **Non-duplication** — existing SubhForge commands, agents, skills, rules,
   or deterministic tools do not already cover it adequately.
3. **Source quality** — the upstream source is credible enough for the
   capability being adopted; primary sources are preferred where available.
4. **Provenance** — repository/path, commit or version, license, and local
   adaptation rationale are recorded.
5. **Authority fit** — the skill cannot override accepted PRD, architecture,
   ADR, project rules, or SubhForge lifecycle/reconciliation semantics.
6. **Tool/permission fit** — requested tools and side effects are explicit and
   bounded; hidden or unnecessary write/network authority is rejected.
7. **Context fit** — the skill is concise/on-demand enough that it does not
   create avoidable context/token pressure merely by existing.
8. **Determinism fit** — mechanical correctness that belongs in deterministic
   code is not delegated to prompt guidance merely because an external skill
   does so.
9. **Portability fit** — avoid unnecessary coupling to one model/harness when
   the useful principle can remain portable.
10. **Security/supply-chain review** — copied/adapted prompt instructions,
    scripts, dependencies and update behaviour are inspected rather than
    trusted automatically.
11. **Validation** — the adapted skill has a focused test, review scenario,
    architecture spike, or dogfood case proving the intended behaviour/value.
12. **Removal test** — if removing the skill would not materially weaken
    quality, reliability, clarity, or cost, it has not earned global scope.

Possible outcomes are:

`REJECT | WATCH | ADAPT | ADOPT_AS_IS`

`ADOPT_AS_IS` should be rare; local adaptation is preferred when workflow or
authority assumptions differ.

### MCP and external-tool integration principle

MCP servers and other external tool integrations may materially improve
SubhForge when they provide **authoritative current knowledge, reliable tool
access, or bounded external actions** that would otherwise be fragile,
expensive, or repeatedly reimplemented.

They are not a goal by themselves.

SubhForge must **not** accumulate MCP servers merely because they are
available. Every server must earn its place by solving a real SubhForge or
project-delivery need with acceptable security, reliability, context, and
operational cost.

Use this capability distinction:

```text
SKILL
  reusable engineering knowledge / method

MCP OR EXTERNAL TOOL
  live knowledge, repository/service access, bounded external action

DETERMINISTIC SUBHFORGE CODE
  lifecycle, state, validation, graph/evidence/mechanical correctness

HUMAN
  consequential product / architecture / risk decisions
```

MCP is therefore a **tool/transport boundary**, not an authority boundary.
The durable source of truth is the system of record assigned to that class of
information by SubhForge's authority model:

- **Git** for durable product/architecture/code authority and approved
  repository audit artifacts;
- **Jira** for the intended LARGE-project operational work graph (Idea/Research
  items, Epics, Features, Specs, Bugs, work-item state, acceptance criteria,
  declared dependencies, verification state, and implementation links), subject
  to the architecture review validating the Jira integration;
- external MCP/tool output is never authoritative merely because an agent
  observed it.

An MCP response or transient model conversation must never become the only
durable copy of accepted state. An MCP-mediated write becomes durable only when
the assigned system of record confirms the mutation.

Loss of an MCP integration must not make authoritative state unrecoverable.
For a system of record such as Jira, SubhForge must retain a viable native or
alternative access/recovery path (for example, Jira's own UI/API/export
capabilities) rather than creating a second shadow authority merely to survive
an MCP outage. Loss of the external service itself is a distinct availability
risk that the review must assess explicitly.

#### Security-first MCP admission gate

Security is a first-class acceptance criterion for every MCP integration.

A server is considered only when all applicable checks are satisfied:

1. **Need and value** — the server addresses a concrete capability gap or
   materially improves correctness, freshness, safety, developer effort, or
   cost.
2. **Trust/source quality** — ownership, maintenance quality, distribution
   source, version/update model, and relevant security history are understood.
   Prefer official/vendor-maintained servers for privileged platforms where
   practical.
3. **Least privilege** — credentials and permissions grant only the resources
   and actions required for the intended use case.
4. **Read-only by default** — knowledge/query access is preferred. Write,
   destructive, deployment, security-policy, financial, data-mutating, or
   production-impacting operations require an explicit justified need and
   stronger controls.
5. **Explicit tool allowlist** — expose only the required MCP tools/namespaces;
   do not make an entire server surface available merely for convenience.
6. **Human gate for consequential actions** — high-impact writes require the
   appropriate explicit human authorization before execution.
7. **Credential and secret isolation** — unrelated servers should not share
   broad credentials; secrets must not be embedded in prompts, artifacts, or
   durable logs.
8. **Untrusted-output posture** — MCP tool descriptions, returned text, fetched
   documents, repository content, and service responses are treated as
   potentially adversarial model input. They cannot override project authority
   or SubhForge policy.
9. **Prompt/tool-poisoning resistance** — external instructions are data unless
   explicitly trusted by SubhForge; tool/schema changes that can alter agent
   behaviour must be reviewed rather than silently inherited.
10. **Version/update control** — prefer pinned or otherwise reviewable server
    versions/configuration where practical; material tool/schema/permission
    changes require re-evaluation.
11. **Fail-safe degradation** — unavailable, malformed, unauthorized, or
    suspicious MCP behaviour fails closed for the affected capability and
    exposes a useful diagnostic; it must not trigger semantic guessing.
12. **Auditability** — meaningful external actions should be attributable to
    the SubhForge operation, target, tool/server, result, and authorization
    context using the minimum useful logging.
13. **Context/tool-cost fit** — server/tool discovery and results must not create
    disproportionate token, latency, or tool-selection noise.
14. **Determinism fit** — do not move mechanical correctness into MCP merely
    because a tool can perform it; deterministic local validation remains local
    where that produces stronger guarantees.
15. **Validation** — representative safe-path and denied/failure-path behaviour
    is proven through focused tests, an architecture spike, or dogfooding before
    broad reliance.

Possible review outcomes are:

`REJECT | WATCH | READ_ONLY_PILOT | APPROVE_BOUNDED`

`APPROVE_BOUNDED` means approval for a declared use case, tool allowlist,
permission scope, and environment. It is not blanket approval of every tool the
server exposes.

#### Initial candidate classes

The Architecture Fitness Review may evaluate high-value candidates such as:

- **knowledge/documentation MCPs** for current platform guidance (for example,
  Microsoft/.NET/Azure documentation);
- **repository MCPs** for GitHub operations;
- **Jira / work-management MCPs or APIs** for bounded work-graph query and
  mutation, including failure/recovery behaviour and minimal-context retrieval;
- **cloud/platform MCPs** for AWS or Azure inspection and carefully bounded
  operations;
- **human-in-the-loop/notification MCPs** where they reduce interaction
  friction without becoming authority.

These are **candidates, not release requirements**. The review should look for
other credible options and should reject any candidate whose value does not
justify its trust surface.


## 2.2 Accepted Directional Architecture --- October 1, 2026

The following decisions capture the design direction agreed before the formal
Architecture Fitness Review. They are **not permission to skip the review**.
They are strong review inputs that must be explicitly evaluated against the
failure model, complexity guardrail, external-practice reconnaissance, spikes,
and dogfood evidence.

If the formal review changes one of these directions, the review must record
the change as an explicit `MODIFY` or `REMOVE` decision with reasoning and
validation. The design must not silently drift back to an older assumption.

### SubhForge's operating model

SubhForge is intended to be **Subhadeep's personal software-delivery
orchestration system**, not merely a coding agent and not a generic engineering
platform for other teams.

Coding/review/research models are replaceable workers inside the system.
SubhForge owns the lifecycle, authority boundaries, context construction,
mechanical correctness, work sequencing, recovery, and evidence needed for
Subhadeep to deliver reliable software with low operational friction and
controlled AI cost.

The machine should absorb ceremony. Subhadeep should normally interact at
high-value points:

`answer important questions -> review rationale -> approve consequential decisions -> build/verify`

He should not need to manually coordinate hidden lifecycle mechanics.

### Three authority/execution planes

#### 1. Product Truth --- Git

Git stores the small set of durable project knowledge that defines what the
product is and how it is fundamentally built:

- canonical discovery document;
- canonical PRD;
- canonical architecture document;
- source code and tests;
- approved reconciliation verdict/audit documents when a durable decision
  record is required.

Canonical documents are updated **in place** through reviewed commits/PRs.
Do not create `discovery-v2.md`, `prd-v2.md`, or `architecture-v2.md`
merely to represent normal evolution; Git already provides version history.

#### 2. Operational Work Graph --- Jira

For LARGE projects, the intended operational planning/execution graph lives in
Jira rather than being expanded into a large Markdown hierarchy in the
repository.

The graph may contain:

- Idea / Research items;
- Epics;
- Features;
- Specs;
- Bugs;
- lifecycle/status;
- acceptance criteria;
- declared dependencies;
- implementation/PR links;
- verification state and evidence references where appropriate.

Epic/Feature/Spec work items are not duplicated into repository Markdown by
default. Jira must remain navigable by Subhadeep and retrievable by SubhForge
on demand.

The architecture review must validate Jira's fitness, access model, failure
behaviour, portability/recovery expectations, context cost, and bounded write
semantics before this direction becomes implementation-final. If Jira fails the
fitness review, preserve the **authority-plane separation principle** rather
than silently returning to thousands of repository planning artifacts.

#### 3. Execution --- SubhForge + agents + Git/CI + bounded integrations

Agents reason and act against the two authority planes through bounded
SubhForge capabilities. Git/CI provides code/review/build evidence. Jira is
accessed through a controlled tool boundary rather than allowing agent prompts
to embed Jira-specific mechanics everywhere.

Derived views such as a work plan or status summary are **projections**, not a
third authority store. They must be regenerable from durable authority.

### Lifecycle and agent responsibility

The intended LARGE workflow is:

1. **Ideation / discovery**
   - begin from a vague idea;
   - actively question Subhadeep to shape the idea;
   - persist the evolving canonical discovery document in Git;
   - record unresolved questions or research needs as traceable Idea/Research
     work in Jira.

2. **Research**
   - Subhadeep or a research agent resolves the recorded research/question
     items;
   - resolved items are closed and their accepted conclusions are reflected in
     the canonical discovery document;
   - PRD creation does not begin while material discovery questions remain
     unresolved.

3. **PRD**
   - derive a comprehensive PRD from accepted discovery, consulting linked
     research/work items only as needed;
   - PRD interaction is two-way: the PRD agent may ask material requirement
     questions and Subhadeep may question the rationale behind proposed
     requirements;
   - approval means material functional ambiguity has been removed sufficiently
     for architecture.

4. **Architecture**
   - read discovery + PRD and actively close technical gaps;
   - use relevant engineering skills/practices where they materially improve
     the design;
   - propose the project's protected architectural invariants;
   - architecture interaction is two-way: both the architect and Subhadeep may
     question rationale;
   - the architecture PR is reviewed and explicitly approved before planning.

5. **Planning / decomposition**
   - only after PRD and architecture authority are accepted;
   - decompose the product into Epic -> Feature -> Spec work items in Jira;
   - the Planner may reason about decomposition and implementation sequencing,
     but it must **not invent missing product or architecture intent**;
   - if a material ambiguity survives into planning, planning stops and routes
     the gap back to the correct upstream authority;
   - Subhadeep may always question the Planner's rationale even though the
     Planner should not need to interrogate him for unresolved requirements.

6. **Work plan**
   - produce a human-readable, regenerable implementation projection from the
     accepted work graph and current dependency state;
   - the work plan does not become competing authority.

7. **Bootstrap / project initialization**
   - establish the implementation repository/project foundations required by
     the approved architecture.

8. **Implementation / verification / review**
   - implement Specs end to end;
   - update durable work-item state after accepted implementation transitions;
   - when all required Specs for a Feature are complete, verify the Feature as
     an integrated capability before human Feature acceptance;
   - when all required Features for an Epic are complete, verify the Epic as a
     human-evaluable outcome before human Epic acceptance;
   - human-discovered defects are recorded as Bugs under the relevant work
     scope and become targeted inputs to diagnose/fix;
   - Epic-level human acceptance should be late evidence, not the first place
     integration defects are expected to surface.

### Decomposition semantics and truthful dependency graph

Use:

`Product -> Epics -> Features -> Specs`

with the following intent:

- **Epic** --- a manageable, human-verifiable end-to-end outcome. Roughly 5--8
  Epics is a useful heuristic for projects at SubhForge's expected scale, not a
  hard quota.
- **Feature** --- a human-verifiable capability within an Epic. A Feature may or
  may not own a full E2E path, but it must have meaningful integration-level
  verification intent.
- **Spec** --- the smallest implementation-ready behavioral slice that an agent
  can implement end to end with its own focused verification/unit-test
  expectations.

Cross-cutting foundations should be deliberately surfaced early when required
by the architecture, but dependencies must remain **truthful**.

The dependency model is a **DAG**, not an artificial linked list.

The work plan may therefore cross Epic and Feature boundaries when the true
dependency graph requires it. Starting an Epic does **not** imply that every
Feature in that Epic must finish before eligible work in another Epic can
begin.

When multiple work items are equally eligible, prefer continuity in the current
Feature/Epic to avoid unnecessary context switching, but do not invent a false
dependency merely to force that continuity.

### Protected Architecture Invariants

Each approved project architecture may declare a small set of
**Protected Architecture Invariants**: foundational decisions that ordinary
feature delivery must not erode accidentally.

Examples may include persistence/data-ownership boundaries, domain boundaries,
eventing/consistency model, deployment topology, security/trust boundaries,
observability architecture, CI/CD/build foundations, or testing philosophy.
The exact invariants are project-specific and must be justified by the
Architect.

Normal planning, implementation, fixing, and reconciliation **cannot modify a
Protected Architecture Invariant**.

If a proposed requirement/change conflicts with one, SubhForge stops with:

`PROTECTED_ARCHITECTURE_CONFLICT`

and identifies the affected invariant and reason. The next step is a deliberate
human architectural decision, not automatic repair.

v0.2 does **not** build an automated architecture-rebaseline subsystem for this
exceptional case.

If Subhadeep explicitly concludes that an invariant was genuinely wrong or must
change:

1. perform an explicit architecture review/revision;
2. update the canonical `architecture.md` through a reviewed PR;
3. explicitly approve the new architecture authority;
4. resume normal reconciliation against that newly accepted authority.

Thus protected invariants are immutable to normal workflow, but not impossible
to change through an explicit human-authorized architectural revision.

### Reconciliation --- analyze -> approve -> apply

Reconciliation is intentionally split so semantic impact analysis cannot
silently mutate operational truth.

The intended flow is:

1. capture and resolve the change/discovery questions;
2. update the canonical discovery and PRD as required through normal review;
3. Architect evaluates the change against the current architecture and
   Protected Architecture Invariants;
4. if a protected invariant is violated, stop with
   `PROTECTED_ARCHITECTURE_CONFLICT`;
5. otherwise update the canonical architecture when the accepted change
   legitimately requires an adaptable architectural change;
6. Planner analyses the accepted authority delta and produces a reconciliation
   verdict;
7. the verdict identifies affected Epics/Features/Specs, state/evidence impact,
   dependency changes, and whether work should be retained, repurposed,
   superseded/closed, or newly created;
8. Subhadeep may question the Planner's rationale and explicitly approves the
   verdict;
9. only then does the reconciliation executor create/update/close Jira work
   items and relationships;
10. regenerate status/work-plan projections from the resulting durable state.

A partially applied Jira mutation must be diagnosable and safely recoverable.
The Planner's analysis and the executor's mutation authority remain separate.

### Focused context propagation

The Planner is responsible for preserving SubhForge's context-size discipline
while creating the work graph.

Later operations should assemble only the context required for the target work,
normally from:

- relevant canonical Git authority;
- the target Jira item;
- required parent/ancestor context;
- declared dependencies/contracts;
- relevant code/implementation surface;
- active reconciliation obligations;
- required verification evidence.

An agent should be invokable from a concrete work-item ID without loading broad
project history.

### Status is a capability, not a heavyweight new agent

SubhForge should provide a deterministic status capability (building on the
planned `/status` direction) for a Project/Epic/Feature/work item.

It should be able to answer, from the durable work graph:

- completed / active / remaining item counts;
- current executable work;
- next eligible item(s);
- blockers and dependency conditions;
- why a later item is not yet eligible;
- approximately how many executable graph items precede another item on the
  current plan, where that answer is meaningful.

Avoid false precision such as "63% complete" merely from counting unequally
sized Specs. A percentage is allowed only when a defensible sizing/weighting
basis exists.

The status reasoning layer may explain *why* the graph produces an answer, but
the underlying state/progress calculation should remain deterministic wherever
possible.

### Review obligations created by this direction

The Architecture Fitness Review must explicitly validate at least:

- whether Jira is fit as the operational work-graph system of record for this
  personal workflow;
- bounded Jira/MCP/API read/write authority and failure behaviour;
- Git <-> Jira authority separation without mirrored competing truth;
- recoverability when the Jira MCP/tool path is unavailable;
- safe/recoverable partial Jira mutation;
- truthful DAG modelling and deterministic work-plan traversal;
- direct work-item-ID context construction within context budgets;
- Planner gap routing rather than requirement invention;
- Protected Architecture Invariant enforcement;
- reconciliation's analyze -> approve -> apply boundary;
- deterministic status projections;
- Feature/Epic verification and human-acceptance handoffs.

These are architecture-review cases, not excuses to add enterprise machinery.
The Complexity Guardrail remains fully applicable.


## 3. Governing Decision Priorities

When architecture, validation, scope, and schedule compete, use:

1. **Correctness and resilience**
2. **Required validation and dogfooding**
3. **Scope discipline and simplicity**
4. **Schedule**

The schedule is a planning constraint, not an architectural correctness
constraint. If a justified change materially improves a required
invariant or addresses a credible failure mode and survives the
simplicity/cost test, implement it even if the plan must move.

Dogfooding must not be weakened merely to preserve a target date.

**December 1, 2026 is a protected target for starting VidyaBeacon, not an
unconditional architectural override.** It may move only through an
explicit decision; it must never drift silently.

A schedule re-plan is triggered when any of the following occurs:

- the Architecture Fitness Review returns `REWORK` and the accepted
  correction materially consumes the planned v0.2 implementation/dogfood
  window;
- `v0.2.0-rc1` has not met its frozen exit criteria by
  **November 8, 2026**;
- `v0.2.0-rc3` has not met its frozen exit criteria by
  **November 18, 2026**, or dogfooding has produced an accepted
  `ADD`/`MODIFY` that is not yet implemented.

At that trigger, explicitly decide whether the remaining required
architecture and dogfood evidence can still be completed responsibly
before December 1. If not, move the VidyaBeacon start date rather than
weakening architecture or validation.

## 4. Complexity Guardrail

Resilience is not permission to gold-plate SubhForge. A mechanism must
earn its place by addressing a required capability, correctness
invariant, credible failure mode, recoverability need,
portability/harness risk, cost/token problem, observability problem, or
material solo-developer burden.

"Industry best practice" or "architecturally cleaner" alone is not
sufficient.

## 5. Failure and Adversarial Model --- Primary Review Lens

For every credible failure determine: damage, required invariant, owning
capability, established practice, smallest robust implementation,
deterministic/AI/human responsibility, proof, and fallback behaviour.

Before deciding review depth, rank the catalogue using a lightweight
**Likelihood × Damage** assessment. The ranking exists to focus
investigation, not to remove lower-ranked failures from coverage.

Rules:

- deeply analyse the highest-risk failures first;
- select architecture spikes only where material uncertainty remains
  after reasoning;
- target roughly **4–5 highest-value spikes** for the initial review;
- a spike should consume **no more than one normal working session**
  unless evidence demonstrates that a larger experiment is necessary;
- lower-ranked failures still remain in the coverage matrix and
  dogfood/adversarial catalogue where relevant.

Initial catalogue:

| Failure mode | Primary concern |
|---|---|
| Context drift or irrelevant context overwhelms reasoning | Bounded context / persistent truth |
| Required governing context is omitted | Deterministic context construction / dependency integrity |
| Git product authority and Jira operational state silently disagree | Authority-plane separation / reconciliation / diagnostics |
| Jira mutation is only partially applied or replayed after interruption | Transaction boundary / idempotency / recovery |
| Jira/MCP access is unavailable and the project cannot be understood or resumed | External-tool degradation / recoverability |
| Planner invents missing requirement/architecture intent instead of routing upstream | Bounded AI authority / authority integrity |
| Normal reconciliation changes a Protected Architecture Invariant | Architecture authority / fail-closed enforcement |
| Work-plan sequencing invents dependencies that do not exist in the real DAG | Work-graph fidelity / deterministic traversal |
| Status projection is stale or reports false precision | Deterministic projection / observability |
| LLM corrupts machine-significant metadata | Canonicalization / schema validation |
| Duplicate or unresolved artifact IDs | Identity / graph integrity |
| Half-applied mutation after interruption/tool failure | Safe mutation / recovery / resumability |
| Command mutates authority outside permission | Bounded mutation authority |
| Repeated verify/fix/diagnosis loop | Retry budget / escalation |
| Spec, code, tests, and evidence silently diverge | Verification / reconciliation |
| Provider contract changes after consumers progress | Impact analysis / reconciliation |
| Evidence remains trusted after claim/code change | Evidence invalidation |
| Reconciliation graph is malformed | Fail-closed safety + early diagnostics |
| Operation becomes BLOCKED without explanation | Observability / diagnostics |
| Model produces plausible but mechanically invalid output | Deterministic validation |
| Harness/model/tool fails mid-operation | Failure containment / resume |
| Kilo-specific behaviour leaks into lifecycle semantics | Harness boundary |
| Kilo becomes unavailable or unsuitable | Portability / recoverability |
| SubhForge itself becomes unavailable | Project independence |
| Token/context use grows unexpectedly | Budget/context controls |
| Repeated AI attempts consume money without progress | Retry/cost controls |
| Governance costs more than value protected | Solo-developer fitness |
| STANDARD regresses while LARGE evolves | Compatibility isolation |
| Routine validation requires long smoke runs for small changes | Testability / feedback latency / developer burden |
| Over-privileged, poisoned, unstable, or unnecessary MCP/tool integration influences the agent or external systems | MCP/tool integration security / least privilege / supply-chain / authority isolation |

The catalogue is extensible; discovering new credible failure modes is
expected.

## 6. Required System Invariants

### Persistent Truth

Important project truth must not depend primarily on transient LLM context.

For the intended LARGE workflow, durable truth is deliberately split by
authority class:

- canonical product/architecture/code authority is inspectable and versioned in
  Git;
- operational Epic/Feature/Spec/Bug state and dependency relationships are
  inspectable in the approved work-graph system of record (intended: Jira);
- derived status/work-plan output is reconstructable and never the sole durable
  copy of state.

Accepted truth must remain inspectable outside a model session and must not
exist only in Kilo conversation state, MCP output, or generated prompt context.

### Authority-Plane Separation

Every important class of information has one declared authoritative home.
SubhForge must not create mirrored Markdown/Jira lifecycle or dependency state
that can drift into two competing truths.

Cross-plane references must be explicit enough to diagnose disagreement and
reconciliation obligations.

### Protected Architecture Authority

Protected Architecture Invariants cannot be changed by ordinary planning,
implementation, fix, or reconciliation operations.

A detected conflict fails closed as `PROTECTED_ARCHITECTURE_CONFLICT` and
requires explicit human architectural review. v0.2 does not automate the
exceptional architecture-rebaseline process.

### Truthful Work Graph and Derived Sequencing

Epic/Feature/Spec dependencies represent real prerequisite relationships as a
DAG.

Implementation sequencing, work plans, and status are derived from that graph.
They may cross hierarchy boundaries and must not mutate the dependency graph
merely to produce a convenient linear schedule.

### Interrogable Reasoning with Bounded Question Authority

Subhadeep may question the rationale of the responsible reasoning agent at any
important design/planning stage.

Agents may actively ask questions only where the stage owns unresolved
ambiguity. Once PRD and architecture are accepted, the Planner must route
material requirement/architecture gaps upstream rather than inventing intent or
reopening product discovery implicitly.

### Deterministic Mechanical Correctness

Where software can reliably enforce correctness, the LLM should not be
the sole enforcement mechanism. Candidates include identity, schemas,
references, graph integrity, lifecycle transitions, evidence integrity,
retry/budget accounting, mutation rules, and canonical serialization.

### Bounded AI Authority

Each operation has explicit permitted reasoning and mutation scope.
Implementation/repair operations cannot silently redefine accepted
product or architecture authority.

### Bounded Context

Operations receive the smallest context sufficient for correctness:
governing authority, declared dependencies, parent scope, relevant
implementation surface, active reconciliation obligations, and required
evidence.

### Safe Failure

Models, tools, scripts, metadata, external services, and the harness can
fail. Failure must be contained and must not silently corrupt accepted
truth.

### Diagnosable Failure

Blocked/failed operations expose enough causal information for
Subhadeep + AI to understand what happened without routine excavation of
SubhForge internals.

### Recoverability and Resumability

Interrupted work is safely restartable or resumable from persisted state
without guessing what completed.

### Project Independence

A SubhForge-managed repository remains understandable,
version-controlled, and recoverable if SubhForge or Kilo is unavailable.

### Compatibility Isolation

LARGE evolution must not accidentally alter established STANDARD
behaviour.

### Fast Validation Feedback

SubhForge must provide layered validation so ordinary implementation and
hardening changes can be checked with focused deterministic tests and
cheap static/dry gates. FAST smoke is an integration checkpoint. FULL
smoke is a release/major-framework-change qualification gate, not the
routine inner development loop.

The architecture should make important mechanical rules testable without
requiring model execution or replaying the whole lifecycle. A design that
forces a 20–30 minute FULL smoke for small changes creates operational
friction that will discourage frequent validation and is therefore an
architecture/testability problem, not merely an inconvenience.

## 7. Architecture Review Depth

**DEEP:** lifecycle/state correctness; bounded authority/mutation;
deterministic invariants; safe mutation/failure/recovery/resume;
observability/actionable diagnostics.

**MEDIUM:** bounded context; dependency/impact/reconciliation;
retry/token/time/cost controls.

**QUICK VALIDATION:** artifact ownership; Kilo/harness coupling;
portability/disaster recovery; provider independence; solo-developer
ceremony.

Depth is risk-driven. Promote any area when evidence reveals material
risk.

## 8. Deterministic vs Probabilistic Responsibility

Conceptual trust boundary:

```text
PROBABILISTIC / SEMANTIC
requirements reasoning
architecture trade-offs
decomposition
implementation reasoning
diagnosis hypotheses
semantic review
        |
        v
DETERMINISTIC / MECHANICAL
schema validation
artifact identity
dependency integrity
lifecycle transition rules
mutation boundaries where enforceable
evidence bookkeeping
retry/budget accounting
graph operations
work-item eligibility / DAG traversal
status/work-plan projections where mechanically derivable
canonicalization where required
```

Human judgement remains authoritative for genuine product, architecture,
ambiguity, escalation, approval of reconciliation verdicts, and any explicit
revision of Protected Architecture Invariants.

Semantic decomposition remains probabilistic reasoning, but the persisted work
graph, declared dependencies, lifecycle transitions, and derived eligibility
must pass deterministic checks before mutation where practical.

The review must identify important properties currently dependent on
probabilistic behaviour that could reasonably be deterministic.

## 9. Observability and Troubleshooting

> **Kilo owns harness/runtime telemetry. SubhForge owns domain/lifecycle
> observability.**

Kilo logs are useful evidence, but not by themselves SubhForge's
observability model.

SubhForge should expose: operation; target
project/artifact/Spec/reconciliation; start/result state; deterministic
gates and outcomes; selected context where relevant; harness/model
invocation where relevant; proposed/committed mutations; block reason;
retry/repair attempt; evidence changes; and causal failure chain.

Seek the **minimum useful implementation** for Subhadeep + AI. Do not
assume a heavyweight telemetry platform. Structured lifecycle events,
correlation IDs, deterministic diagnostics, and reuse of Kilo telemetry
may be sufficient if fitness tests prove it.

## 10. Harness Boundary

Kilo is the primary execution harness for v0.2.0, but Kilo should not
become SubhForge's architecture.

Core lifecycle semantics, authority planes, work-item schemas, dependencies,
evidence, reconciliation, and deterministic correctness should avoid
unnecessary Kilo-specific coupling where a small explicit boundary can isolate
the harness.

The same principle applies to Jira access: Jira may be the selected operational
system of record without allowing Atlassian/MCP call shapes to become
SubhForge's lifecycle semantics.

> **Migration-friendly architecture, not multi-harness product
> support.**

v0.2.0 does not need multiple adapters merely to demonstrate
abstraction.

### Model / Harness Behaviour Drift Canary

A provider or harness update can change behaviour even when SubhForge
code has not changed.

Maintain one **small behavioural compatibility canary** that exercises a
minimal representative SubhForge path and checks deterministic
expectations such as artifact shape, required handoff behaviour,
mutation boundaries, and lifecycle outcome.

The canary should be cheap enough to run:

- when the configured model/provider materially changes;
- after a significant Kilo upgrade;
- during release qualification; and
- optionally from `/doctor` as a compatibility check.

The canary baseline is built during rc1 implementation, so it exists
before dogfooding begins, and it is part of the rc1 exit criteria.

This is not a replacement for dogfooding. It is an early-warning
mechanism for external behavioural drift.

## 11. Architecture Spikes and Fitness Tests

Do not keep the review paper-only where important decisions depend on
uncertain runtime behaviour. Spikes are reserved for the highest-risk
uncertainties after the Likelihood × Damage ranking.

The initial review should normally select only **4–5 spikes**, each
capped at **one working session**. Increase that only when the
experiment itself reveals a justified reason.

Candidate questions include:

- Can Kilo expose enough information/hooks for SubhForge lifecycle
  correlation?
- Can mutation boundaries be deterministically enforced at acceptable
  complexity?
- Can interrupted execution be safely detected and resumed/restarted?
- Can semantically valid metadata be canonicalized before persistence?
- Can context be assembled from declared Git authority + a bounded Jira
  work-item/ancestor/dependency slice without broad project history?
- Can Jira support the required bounded create/read/update/link/query workflow,
  including interruption recovery and useful native fallback, without a shadow
  state store?
- Can truthful DAG traversal produce deterministic next-work and status
  projections across Epic/Feature boundaries?
- Can Protected Architecture Invariants be mechanically guarded against normal
  reconciliation/implementation mutation?
- Can Kilo-specific execution behaviour be isolated behind a small
  boundary?
- Can graph corruption be detected before reconciliation begins?
- Can retry exhaustion produce deterministic escalation instead of
  another model attempt?

Each spike records:

- question;
- linked failure mode(s);
- hypothesis;
- smallest experiment;
- observed result;
- architectural implication;
- `KEEP`, `MODIFY`, `REMOVE`, `ADD`, or further investigation.

A spike exists to resolve architectural uncertainty. It does not become
production code by default.

## 12. Architecture Decision Method

External-practice reconnaissance is a normal input to the decision method,
not an optional afterthought. A review case should prefer learning from a
credible existing solution before inventing a bespoke one, while still
challenging that solution's assumptions against SubhForge.

For each reviewed capability record:

1. **Requirement**
2. **Invariant**
3. **Failure mode(s)**
4. **Relevant established practice**
5. **Current SubhForge approach**
6. **Observed evidence / spike result**
7. **Complexity and cost**
8. **Decision** — `KEEP`, `MODIFY`, `REMOVE`, or `ADD`
9. **Reasoning**
10. **Validation required**
11. **Impact on existing v0.2 design**

### Additional Rule for `ADD`

Every `ADD` must explicitly name:

- the credible failure or invariant it addresses; and
- the test, fitness check, or dogfood scenario that would fail (or
  remain materially unsafe) without the addition.

If neither can be named, the addition has not earned its place.

## 13. Coverage Matrix

After failure-driven analysis, use architecture areas as a second-pass
coverage safety net: persistent truth; lifecycle/state;
authority/mutation; context; mechanical invariants; safe
failure/recovery/resume; authority-plane separation; Git/Jira consistency;
dependency DAG / impact / reconciliation; derived work-plan and status
correctness; Protected Architecture Invariants; evidence lifecycle;
observability; harness boundary; MCP / external-tool boundaries; cost/retry;
portability; solo-developer fitness; STANDARD/LARGE compatibility.

**Failure-driven analysis is the primary lens; capability coverage is
the safety net.**

## 14. Dogfooding Is the System-Level Test Suite

Dogfooding is not optional schedule padding. It is core evidence
required to trust SubhForge.

### MediBot — Greenfield LARGE

Prove the meaningful lifecycle and authority split:

`ideate/discovery → research closure → PRD → architecture + protected invariants → planner/decomposition → Jira Epic/Feature/Spec graph → work plan/status → implement Spec → verify/review → Feature verification + human acceptance → Epic verification + human acceptance`

The dogfood must demonstrate that canonical product/architecture knowledge
remains in Git while operational work state is queried and mutated through the
approved Jira boundary without generating a parallel Markdown work hierarchy.

Inject selected adversarial failures from the review.

### MediBot + Evaluation Guardrails — Evolution and Reconciliation

Prove:

`change discovery/research → canonical discovery/PRD update → architecture invariant check → planner impact verdict → human approval → Jira apply → refreshed work plan/status → implementation → re-verification`

Exercise both:

- an ordinary accepted change that can be reconciled without changing protected
  invariants; and
- a change that attempts to violate a Protected Architecture Invariant and is
  stopped before Jira work mutation.

Validate analyze -> approve -> apply separation, impact discovery, dependency
recalibration, evidence invalidation, stopping rules, partial-mutation recovery,
and diagnostics.

### Autonomous Market Intelligence — Scale and Context Qualification

Prove larger multi-Epic/multi-Feature/multi-Spec operation, cross-hierarchy DAG
sequencing, direct work-item-ID resume, bounded Jira context retrieval, status
projection, AI/research pipelines, evidence, monitoring, implementation,
verification, and context pressure.

## 17. Operational Versioning and VidyaBeacon Upgrade Path

VidyaBeacon should not consume an implicitly moving SubhForge.

At project start:

- pin VidyaBeacon to an exact tested SubhForge release/tag (initial
  target: `stable_v0.2.0`);
- record the SubhForge version in project metadata;
- do not automatically adopt newer SubhForge behaviour mid-project.

After December 1, SubhForge changes enter VidyaBeacon through an
explicit upgrade decision.

For blocking fixes:

1. fix on the appropriate SubhForge maintenance line (for example
   `v0.2.x`);
2. run SubhForge unit/smoke checks;
3. run the relevant behavioural canary and any dogfood/regression slice
   affected by the fix;
4. only then update VidyaBeacon's pinned SubhForge version.

Non-blocking convenience or architectural improvements should remain on
later SubhForge versions rather than destabilizing an active VidyaBeacon
project.

## 18. Schedule Relationship

Schedule provides planning and urgency; it does not override required
architecture or validation.

If the review concludes `REWORK`, implementation/dogfooding exposes a
justified resilience requirement, or the November 8 (RC1) or
November 18 (RC3) checkpoint is triggered:

1. find the smallest robust solution;
2. implement it when required for trustworthy operation;
3. preserve required dogfooding/release evidence;
4. explicitly re-plan dates if necessary.

Do not hide schedule pressure by weakening safeguards, removing
adversarial testing, accepting dogfood without evidence, or silently
consuming contingency while pretending the original plan still holds.

**The schedule should report engineering reality rather than dictate
it.**

## 19. Architecture Review Outcome

**KEEP** --- architecture fundamentally sound; proceed with identified
hardening.

**MODIFY** --- foundation sound, but specific
boundaries/safeguards/recovery/observability/context/integrations change
before implementation.

**REWORK** --- foundational assumption creates unacceptable correctness,
resilience, recoverability, or maintainability risk; correct it before
proceeding even if schedule changes.

Verdict requires failure analysis, decisions, necessary spikes/fitness
tests, and coverage matrix.

## 20. Release Confidence Goal

The target is not that SubhForge never fails.

The target is that ordinary LLM, tooling, metadata, state, harness, and
workflow failures are:

> **detected → contained → diagnosable → recoverable**

without routinely turning Subhadeep into a SubhForge maintainer.

Practical confidence metric:

> **How often does SubhForge force Subhadeep to understand or repair
> SubhForge internals while trying to build another project?**

Measure it during dogfooding. Keep a lightweight **SubhForge
Intervention Tally** with one entry whenever project delivery stops
because Subhadeep must inspect, repair, or manually work around
SubhForge internals.

For each incident record only:

- dogfood/project and operation;
- failure category;
- whether SubhForge diagnosed it correctly;
- whether recovery was automatic, guided, or manual;
- approximate developer intervention.

The purpose is trend/evidence, not bureaucracy. Repeated incidents in
the same category are architecture/hardening evidence, not noise.

**Release bar for `stable_v0.2.0`:** the final Autonomous Market
Intelligence run requires no manual SubhForge intervention (guided
recovery is acceptable), and no tally category repeats unresolved.

For VidyaBeacon, this number should approach zero.

Also track whether Subhadeep is being forced to manually reconstruct project
status, choose work that SubhForge could derive from the work graph, or inspect
broad project history merely to give an agent context. Repeated occurrences are
architecture failures in orchestration/context design, not acceptable ceremony.

The intended operating model is:

> **Subhadeep is developing VidyaBeacon using SubhForge --- not
> developing VidyaBeacon and SubhForge simultaneously.**

and:

> **SubhForge absorbs lifecycle ceremony; Subhadeep owns consequential product,
> architecture, acceptance, and exception decisions.**
